Project Reflections

Behind the Work

How I approached each assignment, what I created, and what I learned.

01 | Image Editing

For the image editing projects, I created two alternative book covers for Remarkably Bright Creatures by Shelby Van Pelt, one using GIMP and the other using generative AI. I wanted both covers to represent the relationship between Tova and Marcellus, which I feel is the heart of the book. I had never used GIMP before, and I found the learning curve frustrating. Although GIMP gave me considerable control over individual elements, I spent a lot of time figuring out how to remove backgrounds, resize images, and work with typography. I was happy with the finished cover, but the process was more difficult than I expected.

Creating the AI-generated cover using ChatGPT was a completely different experience. Instead of manipulating individual elements, I described what I wanted and refined my prompts based on the results. AI handled the composition and typography well but sometimes struggled with details I thought would be straightforward. Fixing one problem occasionally created another. Although both approaches involved trial and error, I preferred working with generative AI because I could focus on what I wanted the cover to look like rather than learning unfamiliar editing tools. It also took considerably less time.

For the vector graphic, I used Inkscape to design a logo for LS Book Wizards Inc., a fictitious personalized book-selection service inspired by my love of Harry Potter and connecting readers with books. I incorporated my initials, a wizard hat, wand, and open book into the design, experimenting with vector shapes, typography, and colors. I wanted the finished logo to reflect my identity as a librarian and the magic of finding the right book.

02 | Web Design

For the web design quiz assignment, I adapted an existing geography quiz to create my own Generative AI Literacy quiz that I could use in my library instruction. I wrote eight questions combining basic AI knowledge with scenarios that ask students to apply what they know. Using BBEdit and ChatGPT, I modified the HTML and JavaScript to replace the questions, update the answer feedback, and remove the Cheat Mode option. I also used CSS to incorporate PBSC's colors and improve the formatting. I hadn't worked with JavaScript or CSS before, so this project helped me understand how the different files work together to create an interactive learning object.

For the drag-and-drop web design assignment, I created two versions of a drag-and-drop activity in which students sort AI literacy scenarios into three categories: Appropriate AI Use, Verify Before Using, and Inappropriate AI Use. For the traditional version, I modified existing HTML and CSS code, which was tedious and sometimes frustrating. Finding the correct lines to change was often harder than making the changes themselves. For the AI-assisted version, I used ChatGPT to generate the code and revised my prompts until the activity looked and worked the way I wanted.

The traditional version took several hours, while the AI-assisted version took about 20 minutes. I preferred the AI approach because I could focus on designing the activity rather than searching through code. However, working with the code traditionally gave me a better understanding of HTML and CSS. I learned that AI can make creating interactive learning objects much easier, but understanding how the code works is still important.

03 | Video Production

For the video production projects, I created two versions of The Case of the Fake Citation, an educational video teaching students how to identify and verify AI-generated citations. I wanted the videos to have a playful, old-fashioned detective theme while explaining how students can check article titles, authors, journals, and DOIs. I used iMovie for the traditional version and NotebookLM for the AI-generated version, keeping the same topic so I could compare the two production methods.

For the traditional version, I wrote the script, recorded the narration, found photographs, and used iMovie to arrange everything. I experimented with the Ken Burns effect, transitions, captions, and background music, spending considerable time synchronizing the narration with the images. Although I was familiar with iMovie, I learned how to work with multiple audio tracks and adjust the timing of individual elements. The entire process took approximately five hours, but I enjoyed having control over how the video looked and sounded.

For the AI-generated version, I uploaded my script to NotebookLM and used ChatGPT to help develop the prompts. NotebookLM generated the narration and visuals in minutes, but getting the video I wanted was more difficult. My first attempt was too long, and the second lost some of the detective elements I wanted. Although AI was considerably faster, I preferred working with iMovie because I could make individual adjustments rather than regenerate the video and hope for better results. This project showed me that AI can be useful for creating instructional videos quickly, but producing a video that matches my vision sometimes requires more control than AI tools provide.

04 | Digital Animation

For the digital animation project,, I created a 14-second educational animation demonstrating phototropism, the process by which plants grow toward a light source. I used ChatGPT to help select the scientific concept, develop the storyboard and narration, and write a detailed prompt for Google Flow using Omni 1.2 Flash. I chose phototropism because I wanted to illustrate a scientific process where the movement itself would help explain the concept.

I wanted the animation to show a seedling gradually growing toward a stationary lamp rather than simply bending or tilting toward it. My prompt specified how the light rays should fade into view, how the plant's stem should elongate and curve toward the light, and when the narration and text should appear. I also included a visual explanation of how greater cell growth on the shaded side causes the plant to bend toward the light. I was able to generate the entire animation in one attempt, including the narration, movement, and transitions, without needing to make additional edits in a traditional video editor.

Because I was illustrating a scientific concept, accuracy was particularly important. I used Khan Academy resources to learn about phototropism and checked the finished animation to make sure the plant's movement accurately represented the process. This project showed me how a detailed AI prompt can control multiple animation elements while creating an educational video that would have been considerably more difficult for me to produce using traditional animation tools.

05 | Screencasting

For the screencasting projects, I created two instructional videos demonstrating AI features in library research tools. For the traditional version, I used Camtasia to create a screencast introducing students to EBSCO's AI features. For the AI-assisted version, I used InVideo to demonstrate Primo's AI Research Assistant. Both videos were designed to help students understand how to use these tools when conducting research.

I preferred working with Camtasia because I had much more control over the editing process. I could adjust the timing of the narration, add zoom effects to highlight important features, and use mouse-click indicators to help students follow along with the demonstration. Although getting everything synchronized took time, I liked being able to make individual adjustments until the video looked and sounded the way I wanted.

For the AI-assisted version, I recorded the screencast myself and used ChatGPT's integration with InVideo to help with editing. I also used Google AI Studio to generate the narration because of the limitations of InVideo's free version. While I liked how easy it was to generate a professional-sounding narration without recording mistakes, getting the narration synchronized with the video was particularly frustrating. Creating the narration in separate sections also left long stretches without narration, so I eventually recreated it as one continuous recording. Even making relatively small editing changes sometimes took several attempts.

I was completely surprised that using AI didn't save me any time at all. Although AI-assisted editing has potential, I found that it still required considerable technical knowledge and was difficult to use effectively without paying for a subscription. For this project, I preferred Camtasia because it gave me the control I needed to create an instructional screencast that matched what I wanted.

06 | ePortfolio Design

For the ePortfolio design projects, I created two versions of my ePortfolio, one by modifying an existing HTML and CSS template and the other using ChatGPT to generate the code from scratch. I wanted both websites to reflect my professional identity as an academic librarian, so I incorporated the Palm Beach State College Libraries logo and PBSC's green-and-gold color scheme while creating two distinctly different designs.

For the traditional version, I used BBEdit to customize the template, modifying the navigation, fonts, colors, and page layouts. I created separate pages for my projects and adjusted the CSS to make the homepage more consistent. The process involved considerable trial and error, particularly when changes to the HTML or CSS affected elements I hadn't intended to change. Although I used ChatGPT to help troubleshoot problems, I still needed to locate and modify the code myself. Working through these issues helped me better understand how HTML and CSS work together.

For the AI-created version, I used ChatGPT to generate the HTML and CSS based on my design preferences. I chose a more modern, single-page layout with different typography while retaining the PBSC branding. I copied the generated code into BBEdit and continued using it to make adjustments as I refined the website, previewing the changes in Chrome. I found this approach much easier because I could describe the changes I wanted rather than spending time locating and modifying individual lines of code. However, I still needed to test the website, identify problems, and make revisions. I preferred the AI-created version because it gave me more creative control over the design, but both experiences reinforced the importance of understanding how HTML and CSS work together.

Screencasting Project

AI-Assisted Video Reflection

For this assignment, I used InVideo through its integration with ChatGPT to create an instructional screencast demonstrating Primo's AI Research Assistant. I recorded the screencast myself and used AI to generate the narration and add visual effects. The biggest advantage I found was AI-generated narration. When recording my own narration, I often make mistakes or stumble over words, which means having to rerecord sections until I get them right. Using Google AI Studio eliminated those problems and allowed me to generate a professional-sounding voiceover from my script. I also liked being able to connect ChatGPT directly to InVideo and describe the changes I wanted rather than having to learn another editing interface. I can see how these features could be useful for educators who want to create instructional videos but have limited experience with video editing software.

However, creating the video was much more difficult than I expected. The free version of InVideo limited my ability to generate narration, so I had to use Google AI Studio instead. Generating the narration in five separate sections resulted in noticeable differences in the voice. The five recordings were also too short for the video, leaving long stretches without any narration. This meant recreating the narration as one continuous recording. Getting that narration to match the actions on screen was particularly frustrating. I spent considerable time adjusting the timing because the narration frequently described something before or after it appeared on screen. Even getting the pop-up text to display with the correct background color took several attempts. What surprised me most was how little time AI actually saved. Although ChatGPT could make changes directly in InVideo, I still needed to recognize problems and explain how to correct them. For educators without video editing experience, I think this could be frustrating rather than helpful. While AI-assisted video editing has educational potential, my experience showed that it still requires technical knowledge and can be difficult to use effectively without paying for a subscription.
